## Overview

Secondary Infektion is the name researchers gave to a long-running influence operation originating in Russia that, between 2014 and 2020, planted forged documents and fabricated stories across hundreds of websites. In 2020 the analysis firm Graphika identified at least 2,500 pieces of content in seven languages on more than 300 platforms and forums. Almost all of it went unnoticed, with one major exception: leaked UK–US trade documents during the 2019 British general election.

## Background

In May 2019 Facebook removed a small network of accounts "emanating from Russia". Working outwards from them, the Atlantic Council's Digital Forensic Research Lab (DFRLab) uncovered a far larger operation and named it after the Soviet-era Operation INFEKTION, which planted the claim that the United States created AIDS in distant media before amplifying it. The DFRLab judged that its scale, tradecraft and obsession with secrecy pointed to a well-resourced actor, "possibly an intelligence agency", but said open sources could not attribute it to a particular Russian body.

## Methods

- Forgeries were the core: fake letters, tweets and official documents that, if real, would have set Western governments or political groups against each other.
- Examples included a fake tweet attributed to U.S. Senator Ted Cruz accusing British intelligence of meddling in the 2018 midterms, and a letter supposedly from the Spanish foreign minister, whose name was misspelled, claiming that radical opponents of Brexit planned to assassinate Boris Johnson.
- Each story was usually posted by a single-use "burner" account on lightly moderated platforms such as Reddit and Medium, then abandoned.
- Ukraine was its most frequent target. Other themes included Angela Merkel and migration, Emmanuel Macron, the 2018 Winter Olympics and attacks on Kremlin critics.

## Exposure

In October 2019 documents on UK–US trade talks appeared on Reddit and other sites and became a headline issue of the December election campaign. That December, acting on information from Graphika, Reddit banned one subreddit and 61 accounts, and Graphika linked the leak's amplification to Secondary Infektion. Unlike the operation's other stories, the documents showed no sign of being forged; how they were obtained remained unclear. In July 2020 the British government said it was "almost certain" that Russian actors had sought to interfere in the election by amplifying them, without formally blaming the Russian state.

## Legacy

Researchers see Secondary Infektion as evidence of both the ambition and the limits of covert online operations. Its forgeries were mostly ignored or mocked, partly because using a new account for almost every story meant it never built an audience; the one story that broke through was based on genuine material. Graphika noted that it ran in parallel with the Internet Research Agency and the GRU while being "systematically different in its approach".
