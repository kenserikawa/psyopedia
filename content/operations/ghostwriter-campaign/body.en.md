## Overview

Ghostwriter is an influence campaign, active since at least March 2017, that combines hacking with fabricated content to spread anti-NATO narratives in Eastern Europe and, from 2020, narratives hostile to the Polish and Lithuanian governments. Its attribution has evolved: the cybersecurity firm Mandiant links the group behind it, which it tracks as UNC1151, to the Belarusian government with high confidence, while Germany, Poland and the European Union have publicly associated Ghostwriter with the Russian state.

## Background

In July 2020 Mandiant tied together a series of incidents that it assessed, with moderate confidence, to form one campaign aligned with Russian security interests. The operations targeted audiences mainly in Lithuania, Latvia and Poland and portrayed NATO's presence as a threat. Mandiant later linked the campaign to UNC1151, a cyber-espionage group focused on Ukraine, Lithuania, Latvia, Poland and Germany, with defence ministries as its most common government targets, and in November 2021 assessed that its operators were likely located in Minsk.

## Methods

- Operators compromised real news websites or spoofed email accounts to publish falsified articles, quotes, letters and documents attributed to military officials and politicians.
- At least 14 invented personas, posing as local journalists and analysts, cited the material in op-eds on sites that accept user submissions.
- After Belarus's disputed election of August 2020, 16 of 19 operations observed by Mandiant defamed the Polish and Lithuanian governments. In August 2021, during the migrant crisis on Belarus's borders, one pushed a story about a fabricated crime by migrants.
- From June 2021 the campaign also ran hack-and-leak operations, publishing allegedly leaked Polish government communications that Belarusian state television then reported.

## Exposure

On 6 September 2021, ahead of the federal election, the German government said it had reliable information attributing Ghostwriter's phishing attempts against federal and state lawmakers to Russian state actors, specifically the GRU military intelligence service, and warned that they could prepare disinformation campaigns. On 24 September the EU's High Representative declared that some member states had associated Ghostwriter with the Russian state. Poland attributed it "in the first place" to Russian special services while allowing that other actors might be involved. Mandiant, for its part, could not rule out Russian contributions but had found no direct evidence of them.

## Legacy

Ghostwriter showed how espionage and information operations can be fused, using stolen access to lend fabricated content the credibility of real outlets and officials. Mandiant said the case showcases "the accessibility and deniability" of such operations, a point underlined by the diverging attributions to Minsk and Moscow. The activity has continued: in 2025 SentinelLABS described a new campaign against Ukrainian government bodies and Belarusian opposition activists as an extension of Ghostwriter.
