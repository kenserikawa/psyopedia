## Overview

Timber Sycamore was a classified CIA program to train and arm Syrian rebel groups fighting the Assad regime, costing approximately $1 billion per year and becoming one of the most expensive covert operations in CIA history.

## Background

Launched in 2013, the program was a joint effort with Saudi Arabia, which matched CIA funding dollar for dollar.

## Methods

- Training rebel fighters in Jordan and Turkey
- Supplying TOW anti-tank missiles, small arms, and ammunition
- Funneling weapons from Eastern European stockpiles
- Vetting rebel groups (though weapons frequently ended up with jihadist groups)

## Failures

- CIA-armed groups fought against Pentagon-armed groups in multiple instances
- Weapons leaked to al-Nusra Front and ISIS-affiliated groups
- A Jordanian intelligence officer involved in the program shot and killed three Americans at a training facility in 2015
- The program failed to achieve regime change

## Exposure

Revealed through a series of leaks and investigative reporting, particularly by the New York Times in 2016. Trump shut down the program in 2017.

## Legacy

Demonstrated the risks of large-scale covert arms programs — weapons proliferation, blowback, and competing U.S. agencies arming opposing factions.
