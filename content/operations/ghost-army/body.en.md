## Overview

The Ghost Army was the nickname of the 23rd Headquarters Special Troops, a secret United States Army unit that impersonated larger American formations on the battlefields of Western Europe in 1944 and 1945. Using inflatable decoys, recorded sound, fake radio traffic and staged theatrics, roughly 1,100 men repeatedly made German commanders believe that American strength lay where it did not.

## Background

According to the National WWII Museum, the unit grew out of American collaboration with the British on Operation Fortitude and was largely the brainchild of Ralph Ingersoll. It was activated on 20 January 1944 at Camp Forrest, Tennessee, under Colonel Harry Reeder. Many of its soldiers were recruited from art schools, advertising agencies and communications firms; later famous members included the painter Ellsworth Kelly, the photographer Art Kane and the fashion designer Bill Blass.

## Methods

- The 603rd Engineer Camouflage Battalion inflated rubber tanks, trucks and artillery to simulate armoured and artillery units.
- The Signal Company, Special ran "spoof radio", mimicking the traffic of real divisions.
- The 3132nd Signal Service Company practised sonic deception, projecting recorded sound effects through loudspeakers that could be heard miles away.
- Soldiers sewed counterfeit shoulder patches, painted false vehicle markings and staffed phony headquarters with fake generals to fool enemy spies.
- The 406th Engineer Combat Company provided security and also posed as military police.

## Aftermath

Congress found that the unit carried out 21 full-scale tactical deceptions, beginning with Operation Elephant on 1–4 July 1944, between the First US and Second British Armies in Normandy. Near Metz its men wore the patches of the 6th Armored Division, and during the Battle of the Bulge counterfeit radio traffic masked the movements of George Patton's Third Army towards Bastogne. In its final mission, Operation Viersen in March 1945, the unit simulated two infantry divisions with about 600 inflatable dummies to draw German forces away from the Ninth Army's crossing of the Rhine. A companion unit, the 3133rd Signal Service Company, used sonic deception in Italy in April 1945. Three of the unit's soldiers were killed and dozens injured. A postwar Army analysis said that rarely had "a group of such a few men" had so great an influence "on the outcome of a major military campaign".

## Exposure

The units remained classified for decades. According to the National WWII Museum, declassified files revealed their history in 1996, after which veterans could speak. The Ghost Army Congressional Gold Medal Act (Public Law 117-85) became law on 1 February 2022, and the medal was presented at the Capitol on 21 March 2024 to the 23rd Headquarters Special Troops and the 3133rd Signal Service Company, with three surviving veterans in attendance.
