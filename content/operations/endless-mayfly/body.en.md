## Overview

Endless Mayfly is the name the Citizen Lab at the University of Toronto gave to a network of lookalike news websites and fake personas that, from at least April 2016 to November 2018, published fabricated articles in the name of real media outlets. Its stories mostly spread false or divisive claims about Saudi Arabia, the United States and Israel. In May 2019 Citizen Lab concluded "with moderate confidence" that Iran or an Iran-aligned actor was responsible, while acknowledging that it had no direct proof that the Iranian state ran it.

## Background

The investigation began in April 2017 with an inauthentic article posted to Reddit. Researchers eventually mapped 135 inauthentic articles, 72 lookalike domains, 11 personas and one fake organisation. In 63 of the 99 articles they could analyse, the stories described events that never happened or quoted statements never made.

## Methods

- Typosquatting: domains imitated outlets such as Haaretz, The Guardian, Bloomberg, The Atlantic, Al Jazeera, Le Soir and Foreign Policy, as well as a German government website, using misspellings, alternative domain endings or lookalike characters.
- Personas posing as journalists, students and activists tweeted the links and messaged real journalists to get them to repeat the stories.
- "Ephemeral disinformation": once an article gained traction, the operators deleted it and redirected the link to the genuine outlet, obscuring the forgery's origin.
- Bot-like amplification: a September 2017 hashtag campaign against Saudi Arabia produced more than 5,500 tweets and retweets, and Twitter later suspended 83% of the 942 accounts behind them.
- Persona bylines on open platforms such as Medium, plus a separate pro-Iran republishing network that spread the content further.

## Exposure

Several stories reached mainstream media. In June 2017 Reuters reported, on the strength of a fake article imitating The Local, that six Arab countries had asked FIFA to strip Qatar of the 2022 World Cup; Reuters later retracted the story. A fabricated Le Soir article claiming Saudi funding for Emmanuel Macron's campaign circulated on French far-right sites, and a fake Foreign Policy article attributed invented criticism of Saudi Arabia to a former CIA director.

In August 2018 Facebook, working with the security firm FireEye, deactivated accounts and pages tied to the republishing network that Endless Mayfly used, and Google and Twitter followed. When Citizen Lab published its findings in May 2019, the network was still active, with evolving tactics.

## Legacy

Measured impact was modest: 44 tracked short links generated 21,686 clicks. Citizen Lab found no evidence of a false flag or a commercial motive, and based its attribution on the fit with Iranian interests, the absence of anti-Iran content and overlaps with Iran-linked republishing sites.
