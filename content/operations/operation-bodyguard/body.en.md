## Overview

Operation Bodyguard was the overall Allied strategic deception plan for 1944, designed to mislead Germany about the time, place and manner of the invasion of north-west Europe. Its best-known component, Operation Fortitude, persuaded the German High Command that the Normandy landings of 6 June 1944 were a diversion and that the main blow would fall on the Pas-de-Calais.

## Background

Britain's London Controlling Section (LCS) had coordinated deception against Germany since a controller was appointed in October 1941. Its draft plan, Jael, became Bodyguard, a name taken from Winston Churchill's remark to Stalin at the Tehran Conference that in wartime truth is so precious "that she should always be attended by a bodyguard of lies". According to a CIA review of the subject, Bodyguard was formally Eisenhower's responsibility, but the LCS coordinated it with full access to Ultra intelligence, and a protocol on Soviet participation was signed in March 1944.

## Methods

- Some 36 subordinate plans threatened Norway, the Pas-de-Calais, the Bay of Biscay and the Mediterranean, alongside a diplomatic offensive aimed at Germany's allies and the neutral states.
- Fortitude North threatened Norway; Fortitude South rested on the First US Army Group (FUSAG), which MI5 describes as a "ghost" force of 11 non-existent divisions under General George S. Patton, apparently poised in Kent and Essex.
- Fake camps, dummy landing craft and simulated radio traffic supported the illusion.
- Double agents run through the Twenty (XX) Committee fed the story to the Abwehr. Juan Pujol García, codenamed Garbo, and his MI5 case officer Tomás Harris invented 27 fictitious sub-agents, and from January 1944 to D-Day Garbo exchanged over 500 radio messages with his German controllers in Madrid.

## Aftermath

On 9 June 1944 Garbo reported that the Normandy landings were a diversion and that the main assault would come further east; the message as received by German intelligence survives at The National Archives (WO 208/4374). According to MI5, the Germans kept two armoured and 19 infantry divisions in the Pas-de-Calais through July and August, and Field Marshal Gerd von Rundstedt overruled a proposal by Erwin Rommel to move them to Normandy. Garbo was awarded the Iron Cross in July 1944 and an MBE that December.

## Legacy

Historians debate how much each element contributed. Mary Kathryn Barbier, speaking on a National WWII Museum podcast, regards Garbo as a contributing factor rather than the sole reason for the deception's success. The story emerged gradually: Anthony Cave Brown's Bodyguard of Lies (1975), which a CIA reviewer found sound in outline but full of errors of detail, Garbo's 1985 memoir, and MI5's release of the Garbo case files in 1999.
