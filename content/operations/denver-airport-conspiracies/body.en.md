## Overview

Denver International Airport (DIA), opened in 1995, has been the subject of persistent conspiracy theories due to its unusual art, Masonic capstone, underground tunnels, and its construction going $2 billion over budget.

## The Evidence Cited

- **Murals** — Artist Leo Tanguma's murals depict apocalyptic scenes including a gas-masked soldier with a sword, dead children, and environmental destruction, followed by scenes of global unity
- **Masonic capstone** — A Freemason time capsule and capstone are embedded in the terminal floor, engraved with the "New World Airport Commission"
- **Underground tunnels** — A massive automated baggage system was built underground (it failed and was replaced, but the tunnels remain)
- **Blue Mustang** — A 32-foot blue horse sculpture with glowing red eyes (nicknamed "Blucifer") killed its creator when a section fell on him
- **Budget overruns** — Originally budgeted at $1.7 billion, final cost was $4.8 billion with no clear explanation

## Theories

- Secret underground bunker for government continuity
- Headquarters for the New World Order
- Murals encode a planned genocide/rebirth scenario
- Tunnels connect to a vast underground complex

## Evidence Status

**The art and architecture are genuinely unusual. The conspiracy interpretations are unsubstantiated.** DIA itself has embraced the theories, installing tongue-in-cheek conspiracy-themed displays during renovations.

## Why It Matters

A fascinating case where genuinely strange aesthetic choices fuel conspiratorial thinking. The airport's decision to lean into the theories is a unique example of institutional engagement with conspiracy culture.
