## Overview

The Gleiwitz incident was a staged attack on the German radio station at Gleiwitz, in Upper Silesia (today Gliwice, Poland), on the evening of 31 August 1939. SD men posing as Poles seized the transmitter and broadcast a brief Polish-language appeal, so that Germany could present its invasion of Poland the next morning as a response to Polish aggression.

## Background

On 22 August 1939 Hitler told his senior commanders that he would provide a propaganda pretext for the war; according to the Nuremberg summary of the speech (document 1014-PS), victory, not law, was what mattered. In his affidavit of 20 November 1945 (document 2751-PS), the SD officer Alfred Naujocks stated that around 10 August Reinhard Heydrich ordered him to simulate an attack on the Gleiwitz station, explaining that "practical proof" of Polish attacks was needed for the foreign press and German propaganda.

## Methods

- Naujocks waited in Gleiwitz with five or six SD men for about two weeks. At noon on 31 August he received the code word, and the attack was set for 8 p.m.
- The team seized the station, broadcast a speech of three to four minutes in Polish over an emergency transmitter, fired some pistol shots and left.
- On Naujocks's instructions, Gestapo chief Heinrich Müller had a man delivered near the station; unconscious but alive, he was laid at the entrance as "evidence". Historian Roger Moorhouse identifies him as Franciszek Honiok, a Silesian farmer with pro-Polish sympathies, who was killed at the scene.
- Müller used the code name "Canned Goods" for such victims. For another staged incident, he described condemned prisoners being given fatal injections and gunshot wounds and left dead in Polish uniforms.

## Exposure

On 1 September Hitler told the Reichstag that there had been 14 frontier incidents the previous night, that "Polish regular soldiers fired on our own territory" and that "since 5:45 a.m. we have been returning the fire". The deception was laid bare at Nuremberg. Erwin von Lahousen of the Abwehr testified on 30 November 1945 that in mid-August 1939 his service had been asked for Polish uniforms and identity papers for an "Undertaking Himmler". He said Admiral Wilhelm Canaris later told him that concentration camp prisoners had been disguised in them for the attack on the Gleiwitz station, a hearsay version that differs from Naujocks's account.

## Legacy

Gleiwitz is a textbook false flag operation staged to fabricate a pretext for war. Much of the detail rests on Naujocks's own testimony, and accounts differ on points such as the wording of the broadcast. Naujocks served about a year of a Danish prison sentence and died in 1966. The wooden transmitter tower still stands and has housed a branch of the Gliwice Museum since 2008.
