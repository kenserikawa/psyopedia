## Overview

Operation Mincemeat was a British deception carried out in the spring of 1943 to hide the true target of the Allied invasion of Sicily. A corpse dressed as a Royal Marines officer was left to drift off Spain carrying forged letters suggesting that the Allies would land in Greece and Sardinia and that Sicily would be only a feint.

## Background

The idea of planting false documents on a dead body appeared in the 1939 "Trout memo" circulated by Naval Intelligence and most likely drafted by Ian Fleming. In 1942 Charles Cholmondeley, an RAF officer attached to MI5, proposed a version of the scheme, and the Twenty (XX) Committee assigned the naval intelligence officer Ewen Montagu to develop it with him. According to the Imperial War Museums (IWM), which holds Montagu's papers, the plan was approved on behalf of Winston Churchill in a letter dated 16 April 1943.

## Methods

- The body was that of Glyndwr Michael, a homeless Welshman who had died after swallowing rat poison; he became "Major William Martin" of the Royal Marines.
- "Pocket litter" built a believable life: a fiancée's photograph, love letters, a receipt for an engagement ring and a theatre ticket stub.
- The key document was a personal letter from Lieutenant General Sir Archibald Nye to General Harold Alexander; another, from Lord Louis Mountbatten, joked about sardines to hint at Sardinia.
- Early on 30 April 1943 the submarine HMS Seraph released the body about a mile off the coast near Huelva, where Germany had a consulate with an Abwehr officer under diplomatic cover.

## Aftermath

A Spanish fisherman found the body that morning. The Spanish authorities held the briefcase before returning it to the British consul on 11 May; according to the IWM, the documents had been covertly opened, photographed and passed to German intelligence. On 14 May Hitler told Admiral Karl Dönitz that Greece and Sardinia had to be reinforced. The 1st Panzer Division was sent from France to Greece and German troops on Sardinia were doubled. The invasion of Sicily, launched on 9 July 1943, achieved surprise, and the island fell in just over a month. A War Office file on the German reaction, dated October 1945, is held at The National Archives (WO 208/3163).

## Legacy

Montagu published his own account, The Man Who Never Was, in 1953, and it was filmed in 1956; Ben Macintyre's 2010 book and a 2022 film revived interest. Historian Michael Howard called it perhaps the most successful single deception operation of the war. According to the National WWII Museum, Glyndwr Michael's real name was added to the grave in Huelva in 1997.
