## Overview

Team Jorge is the codename of an Israel-based private unit that, according to a 2023 investigation coordinated by the French nonprofit Forbidden Stories, sold hacking, social media manipulation and election-interference services to political and corporate clients. Reporters identified its leader as Tal Hanan, described by The Guardian as a former Israeli special forces operative using the alias "Jorge". Hanan did not answer detailed questions and said: "I deny any wrongdoing."

## Background

The revelations were part of Story Killers, a project involving more than 100 journalists from 30 outlets. Three reporters from Radio France, Haaretz and TheMarker posed as consultants for an African country seeking to delay an election and, between July and December 2022, secretly recorded more than six hours of meetings, some at an office in Modi'in, near Tel Aviv. Hanan claimed to have worked on "33 presidential-level campaigns, 27 of which were successful" and quoted fees of 6 to 15 million euros. The consortium warned that not all his claims could be verified and that he may have exaggerated them to win a contract. According to The Guardian, some operations were run through an Israeli company, Demoman International.

## Methods

- AIMS (Advanced Impact Media Solutions), software that Hanan said controlled more than 30,000 fake profiles on platforms such as Twitter, Facebook, Telegram, Gmail and YouTube, some with Amazon accounts and bitcoin wallets. Reporters found profile photos taken from real people.
- Planting material in legitimate news outlets for the bots to amplify, and a "blogger machine" to create websites.
- Hacking demonstrations, such as access to the Telegram accounts of two advisers to William Ruto during Kenya's 2022 election campaign. A consortium reporter later found a stray message from the demonstration on a recipient's phone.
- Sabotage of rivals, such as a claimed sex toy delivery meant to suggest that a politician was having an affair.

## Exposure

The findings were published on 15 February 2023. Meta removed AIMS-linked bots after reporters shared a sample, and connected them to a now-defunct Israeli firm it had banned in 2019. Reporters traced AIMS activity in about 20 countries, mostly in commercial disputes. Leaked emails showed Hanan offering his services to Cambridge Analytica in 2015 and 2017, though neither campaign is known to have gone ahead, and other documents indicated that Team Jorge worked covertly on Nigeria's 2015 presidential election alongside the firm. In France, BFMTV dismissed an anchor after an internal review of segments the consortium linked to Team Jorge, and filed a complaint.

## Legacy

Forbidden Stories stated that there was no definitive proof that Team Jorge was behind attempts to manipulate Kenya's election, and most of its claimed successes remain unverified. Even so, the case offered a rare look inside the private market for disinformation for hire.
