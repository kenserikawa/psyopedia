## Overview

Persistent allegations that the CIA facilitated cocaine trafficking into the United States to fund the Nicaraguan Contras during the 1980s, contributing to the crack cocaine epidemic in American cities.

## Key Evidence

- **Kerry Committee (1989)** — Senator John Kerry's investigation concluded that "members of the U.S. State Department" provided assistance to drug traffickers and that the Contra supply network was used to ship cocaine
- **Gary Webb's "Dark Alliance" (1996)** — San Jose Mercury News series linking CIA-backed Contras to crack cocaine flooding Los Angeles
- **CIA Inspector General report (1998)** — Acknowledged CIA worked with Contra figures known to be involved in drug trafficking and concealed this from the DEA and Congress
- **Mena, Arkansas** — Allegations of drug-running operations at Mena Intermountain Municipal Airport linked to CIA operative Barry Seal

## The Gary Webb Story

Webb's reporting was initially attacked by major newspapers (LA Times, Washington Post, New York Times). He was discredited and forced out of journalism. He died in 2004 of two gunshot wounds to the head, ruled a suicide. Subsequent CIA IG reports vindicated much of his reporting.

## Evidence Status

**Mixed.** The Kerry Committee and CIA IG reports confirm CIA toleration of drug trafficking by Contra allies. The broader claim that the CIA deliberately introduced crack into Black communities remains debated.
