## Overview

The Tartaria conspiracy theory claims that a vast, technologically advanced civilization called the Tartarian Empire existed until the 19th century and was deliberately erased from historical records. Proponents allege that a catastrophic "mud flood" destroyed this civilization and its remnants were reattributed to other cultures.

## Key Claims

- Grand 19th-century buildings (U.S. Capitol, European cathedrals, World's Fair structures) were not built by their attributed constructors but inherited from Tartaria
- A "mud flood" or "reset event" buried the first floors of buildings worldwide (hence many older buildings have basement windows at ground level)
- Star forts across the globe were Tartarian energy-harvesting devices, not military installations
- Free energy technology (atmospheric electricity via ornate architecture) was suppressed after the empire's fall
- Old photographs of "empty cities" prove a population reset after the destruction of Tartaria

## Historical Basis

"Tartary" does appear on historical maps as a vague geographical term for Central and Northern Asia, similar to how "Orient" or "Indies" were used. The Mongol-derived Tatar peoples are real. But the conspiracy extrapolates this into a unified advanced empire.

## Evidence Status

**No credible evidence.** The theory relies on misinterpreted architecture, selective reading of old maps, and ignorance of well-documented construction histories. The "mud flood" phenomenon has mundane explanations (street grade changes, foundation settling, deliberate burial for new construction levels).

## Why It Matters

Tartaria is a uniquely internet-era conspiracy. It emerged primarily on Reddit and YouTube around 2016-2018 and demonstrates how architectural appreciation can be hijacked into conspiratorial narratives. It also reveals widespread unfamiliarity with construction history and urban development.
