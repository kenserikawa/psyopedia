## Overview

Spamouflage, also called Spamouflage Dragon and tracked by Google as DRAGONBRIDGE, is a long-running network of fake and hijacked social media accounts that promotes the positions of the People's Republic of China and attacks its critics. Google's Threat Analysis Group calls it the most prolific influence operation it tracks, and Meta's Ben Nimmo described it in 2023 as "the largest, though unsuccessful, and most prolific covert influence operation" the company knew of.

## Background

The research firm Graphika first documented the network in September 2019, describing a prolific but "ultimately low-impact" spam network on YouTube, Twitter and Facebook that used hijacked or fake accounts to attack Hong Kong protesters. It appeared designed to support the Chinese government and discredit its critics, though Graphika did not attribute it to a specific body. Its signature tactic, as the Canadian government later put it, is to hide pro-Beijing messages among ordinary-looking content.

## Methods

- Accounts bought in bulk or recycled from earlier spam activity, according to Google; most posts carry no political message.
- A small share of political content on Hong Kong, COVID-19, Taiwan, Ukraine and US politics, flattering China and disparaging the United States.
- Activity on more than 50 platforms and forums, according to Meta.
- AI-generated news presenters ahead of Taiwan's January 2024 election.
- Harassment of critics abroad: in 2024 and 2025 Canada documented deepfake videos, sexually explicit AI-generated images and a doxing website aimed at Chinese-language commentators in Canada.

## Exposure

Google says it had disrupted more than 175,000 DRAGONBRIDGE instances by mid-2024; in 2023 alone it disabled over 57,000 YouTube channels, 80% of them without subscribers. In August 2023 Meta removed about 7,700 Facebook accounts and related assets, and said it had linked Spamouflage to "individuals associated with Chinese law enforcement."

In April 2023 the US Justice Department charged 34 officers of China's Ministry of Public Security, assigned to a "912 Special Project Working Group", with creating thousands of fake personas to harass dissidents and spread official narratives. The complaint does not use the Spamouflage name, and the defendants are believed to be in China.

Canada has attributed a 2023 Spamouflage campaign against members of Parliament, and a larger one that began in 2024, to the People's Republic of China "with high confidence."

## Legacy

Spamouflage is a case study in volume over quality. Despite years of takedowns it keeps returning, while Google, Meta and Canada all describe minimal engagement, largely generated by the network's own accounts. Its turn towards harassing diaspora critics has led Canada to call it digital transnational repression.
