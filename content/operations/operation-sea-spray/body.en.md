## Overview

In September 1950, the U.S. Navy sprayed Serratia marcescens bacteria over San Francisco from a ship offshore to test how a biological weapon attack would disperse in a coastal city. The 800,000 residents were unwitting test subjects.

## Methods

- Naval vessel sprayed bacterial aerosol along San Francisco's coastline
- Monitoring stations tracked how far the bacteria spread
- Results showed nearly all 800,000 residents inhaled the bacteria
- The test was one of 239 open-air bioweapon tests conducted over U.S. cities between 1949-1969

## Consequences

- At least one man, Edward Nevin, died of a Serratia marcescens infection at Stanford hospital
- Eleven patients at Stanford were hospitalized with never-before-seen infections
- His family sued the government in 1981 but lost

## Other Tests

- The New York City subway was tested in 1966 (lightbulbs filled with bacteria dropped on tracks)
- Minneapolis was sprayed with zinc cadmium sulfide in the 1950s
- St. Louis low-income neighborhoods were targeted with radioactive particles

## Exposure

Declassified in the 1970s during Church Committee investigations.
