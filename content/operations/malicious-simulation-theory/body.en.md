## Overview

While the philosophical Simulation Hypothesis (Bostrom, 2003) is a respected academic thought experiment, the Malicious Simulation Theory is its conspiratorial cousin: the claim that specific entities deliberately created and maintain our simulated reality as a prison, energy farm, or experiment — and that these controllers can be identified and resisted.

## Key Claims

- Reality is a deliberately constructed simulation designed to harvest energy ("loosh") from human suffering and emotional turmoil
- "Archons" or "Demiurge" entities maintain the simulation, drawing from Gnostic mythology
- Near-death experiences reveal the "trap" — a white light tunnel that recycles souls back into the simulation
- Déjà vu, the Mandela Effect, and glitch phenomena are evidence of simulation errors
- Meditation, psychedelics, and gnosis can allow individuals to "see through" the simulation

## Philosophical vs. Conspiratorial

- **Bostrom's hypothesis**: Statistical argument that advanced civilizations would run ancestor simulations — no claims about purpose or malice
- **Malicious variant**: Specific claims about controllers, energy harvesting, and escape methods — unfalsifiable and untestable

## Cultural Roots

Blends Gnostic cosmology (the Demiurge creating a false material world), The Matrix franchise, Hinduism's Maya (illusion), and Philip K. Dick's philosophical writings.

## Evidence Status

**Unfalsifiable.** By design, any evidence against the theory can be explained as part of the simulation. This makes it a belief system rather than a testable hypothesis.

## Why It Matters

Represents the convergence of ancient religious concepts with modern technology anxiety. The theory is inherently unfalsifiable, making it a useful study in the epistemology of conspiracy thinking.
