## Overview

The Saudi infiltration of Twitter refers to the recruitment of two Twitter employees who, according to US prosecutors, accessed confidential data on accounts critical of the Saudi government in 2014 and 2015 and passed it to Saudi officials. The case led to federal charges in 2019 and, in 2022, to the conviction of the former Twitter manager Ahmad Abouammo by a San Francisco jury.

## Background

Abouammo managed media partnerships for the Middle East and North Africa; Ali Alzabarah was a site reliability engineer. According to the Justice Department's 2019 complaint, between November 2014 and May 2015 a Saudi national, Ahmed Almutairi, and Saudi officials persuaded both men to use their credentials to obtain nonpublic data such as email addresses, phone numbers and IP addresses, which could identify and locate anonymous critics. The appeals court identified the recipient as Bader Binasaker, a close associate of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman.

## Methods

- According to NBC Bay Area's account of the trial, Abouammo received a luxury watch in London in December 2014 and then accessed private information on several accounts, including that of an influential critic of the royal family.
- The Supreme Court's opinion records that the official wired him 300,000 dollars in total; prosecutors said part went through a Lebanese account in his father's name.
- The complaint, as reported by The Register, alleges that from 2015 Alzabarah viewed records for more than 6,000 accounts, including that of the dissident Omar Abdulaziz.

## Exposure

When Twitter confronted Alzabarah in December 2015, he left the United States the next day. Interviewed by the FBI in Seattle in October 2018, Abouammo denied the scheme and emailed agents an invoice that, according to the Supreme Court, he fabricated on the spot. He was arrested in November 2019, while Alzabarah and Almutairi are believed to be in Saudi Arabia.

In August 2022 a jury convicted Abouammo of acting as an agent of a foreign government, money laundering, wire fraud, conspiracy and falsifying records. He was sentenced to 42 months in prison. In December 2024 the Ninth Circuit upheld the convictions but ordered resentencing. In June 2026 the Supreme Court ruled unanimously that the falsification count should have been tried in Seattle and reversed on that point, without addressing the other counts.

## Consequences

The breach has been linked to the case of the aid worker Abdulrahman al-Sadhan, detained in Riyadh in 2018 and sentenced in 2021 to 20 years in prison over an anonymous satirical account he was accused of running. According to The Guardian, Abdulaziz said the FBI had shown him a list of compromised accounts that included it. Abdulaziz's phone had also been infected with Pegasus spyware, according to Citizen Lab.
