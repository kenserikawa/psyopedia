## Overview

Doppelganger is a Russian influence operation, active since at least May 2022, that built copies of European news websites and published fabricated articles under their names, mostly to erode support for Ukraine. Researchers and Meta exposed it in September 2022. In December 2022 Meta attributed it to two Russian companies, Social Design Agency (SDA) and Structura National Technologies, and the U.S. government has since described the campaign as run at the direction of the Russian Presidential Administration.

## Background

The name, used by the EU DisinfoLab in its September 2022 report, refers to the operation's signature tactic: look-alike clones of real media. Working with the investigative group Qurium, the researchers documented imitations of at least 17 media brands, including Bild, The Guardian, 20 Minutes, ANSA and RBC Ukraine, on domains that mimicked the originals' addresses. Meta counted more than 60 impersonating websites and called it the largest and most complex Russian-origin operation it had disrupted since the war in Ukraine began.

## Methods

- Fake articles, videos and polls attributed to real outlets portrayed Ukraine as a corrupt or "Nazi" state, denied the Bucha massacre and warned Europeans that sanctions on Russia would hurt them.
- Links were seeded through short-lived Facebook pages, fake profiles, paid ads, comment spam and petition sites, while redirects and geo-blocking hid the final destination from moderators.
- According to Meta, most activity over two years focused on Ukraine, Germany, France, the United States and Israel, and the operators also used fictitious brands such as Reliable Recent News.
- According to internal documents described by the U.S. Justice Department, a "Good Old USA Project" sought to convince Americans that the war in Ukraine was unwinnable, singled out six swing states and relied on fake accounts, paid influencers and AI-generated content.

## Exposure

Meta's first takedown removed 1,633 Facebook accounts, 703 pages and 29 Instagram accounts. The European Union sanctioned the companies in 2023. In March 2024 the U.S. Treasury sanctioned SDA, Structura and their heads, identified as Ilya Gambashidze and Nikolai Tupikin. On 4 September 2024 the Justice Department announced the seizure of 32 internet domains used by the campaign, alleging that SDA, Structura and ANO Dialog worked under the direction of Sergei Kiriyenko, first deputy chief of staff of the Russian presidential administration.

## Legacy

Meta's later reporting describes Doppelganger as an "advanced persistent threat" among influence operations, yet "crude and largely ineffective" at building authentic audiences: blocking its domains pushed it into clumsier redirects and barely legible ads. Meta also warned of "perception hacking", since such operations may exploit their own notoriety to appear more influential than they are.
